🧩 Heap Patterns — Interview Cheat Sheet

A few ideas cover the whole topic. Learn to spot which one a question wants.

Heap for Top-K / ExtremesTwo Heaps Around the Middle

⛰️ Heap for Top-K / Extremes

A heap always hands you the current smallest (or largest) fast.

The tell (how to spot it): You need the k largest/smallest/most-frequent, or to repeatedly grab an extreme.

How it works: Keep a heap of size k (or of live candidates); push/pop in O(log k). When a key is bounded, bucketing can even hit O(n).

Problems here that use it: Top K Frequent Elements Merge k Sorted Lists

Say this in the interview:
“I’ll keep a heap of the top k so each element costs O(log k) — no need to sort everything.”

⚖️ Two Heaps Around the Middle

Split the data into a low half and a high half so the middle is always in reach.

The tell (how to spot it): You track a median or a value near the center of a changing set.

How it works: A max-heap holds the smaller half, a min-heap the larger half; keep their sizes balanced. The median is at the tops.

Problems here that use it: Find Median from Data Stream

Say this in the interview:
“I’ll balance a max-heap (low half) and min-heap (high half); the median is the top(s) — O(log n) per add.”

Talking through any Heap problem

1. Ask: do I need the extreme repeatedly? That’s the heap signal.

2. Size the heap to k for top-k, or split into two heaps for a median.

3. Remember Python’s heapq is a min-heap — negate values for a max-heap.
